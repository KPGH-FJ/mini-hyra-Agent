### Assistant advice — wood chips & grilling (all 2023-07-01)
- Assistant suggested wood chips: Korean Oak (Quercus acutissima) — mild, sweet flavor with vanilla and caramel hints, traditionally used for Korean BBQ; Post Oak (Quercus stellata) — strong smoky flavor with vanilla, caramel, and a hint of sweetness; Mesquite — robust, savory flavor with hints of leather and tobacco, pairs with spicy and sweet Korean BBQ; Pecan — mild, nutty flavor with hints of sweetness and subtle smokiness, won't overpower other flavors; Cherry — fruity, sweet flavor with hints of almond and subtle smokiness, adds fruity depth.
- Assistant noted wood chip usage tips: soak chips in water at least 30 minutes; use 1/2 to 1 cup per small/medium grill; keep smoking temperature between 225°F and 250°F.
- Assistant suggested hickory tips: start with a small amount (about 1/2 cup) and adjust to taste since hickory is strong; soak hickory chips at least 30 minutes before use; hickory pairs well with robust meats like beef, pork, and lamb — especially ribs, brisket, and sausages; balance hickory's strong flavor with sweet ingredients like brown sugar, honey, or BBQ sauce; add hickory chips during the last 30 minutes to 1 hour of cooking; if hickory is too strong, mix it with oak, apple, or cherry wood; hickory burns best at medium to high temperatures around 250–300°F; store hickory chips in an airtight container to preserve flavor and aroma.
- Assistant suggested general grilling tips: choose the right wood (hickory, oak, mesquite, or applewood; hickory adds a strong, sweet, smoky flavor); soak wood chips or chunks in water for at least 30 minutes so they burn slowly and produce thicker smoke; use a charcoal grill for a more intense smoky flavor than a gas grill; cook low and slow at 225–250°F for deeper, smokier flavor; use a smoke box or chimney to add wood smoke flavor to a gas grill; don't overcrowd the grill so smoke can circulate and penetrate meat evenly; close the lid to trap smoke and heat; be patient — smoking is a slow process; ribs need 4–6 hours of smoke, brisket 10–12 hours; thin blue smoke suggests ideal temperature, thick white smoke means wood is burning too hot.

### Assistant advice — Korean marinades (all 2023-07-01)
- Assistant noted essential Korean BBQ marinade ingredients: gochujang, soy sauce, garlic, ginger, brown sugar, sesame oil, crushed black pepper.
- Assistant noted optional ingredients: gochugaru chili flakes, pineapple or Korean pear juice, green onions, cumin.
- Assistant provided a marinade recipe: 1/2 cup soy sauce, 1/4 cup gochujang, 2 cloves garlic, 1 inch ginger, 2 tbsp brown sugar, 1 tbsp sesame oil, 1 tsp black pepper, optional 1/4 tsp gochugaru; blend until smooth.
- Assistant advised marinating meat at least 2 hours or overnight (8–12 hours) for maximum flavor penetration.
- Assistant suggested marinade variations: Korean-Style Soy Garlic Marinade (soy sauce, garlic, ginger, brown sugar, sesame oil, gochugaru); Gochujang Marinade (gochujang, soy sauce, brown sugar, garlic, ginger, sesame oil); Korean-Style Sesame Oil Marinade (sesame oil, soy sauce, garlic, ginger, brown sugar, gochugaru).
- Assistant suggested Korean BBQ tips: use a combination of gochugaru and gochujang to add depth and heat to sauces and marinades; grill thinly sliced beef, pork, or chicken for a more authentic experience; serve Korean-style BBQ with a side of kimchi for added flavor and nutrition.
