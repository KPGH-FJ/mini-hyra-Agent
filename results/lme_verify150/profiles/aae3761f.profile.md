### Beartooth Highway facts @2023-05-24
- The Beartooth Highway (US-212) is a 68-mile paved scenic road connecting Cooke City, Montana to Red Lodge, Montana through the Beartooth Mountains.
- Generally in good condition from late May to early October, with speed limits of 45-65 mph and elevations over 10,000 feet.
- Assistant advised filling up on gas in Cooke City or Red Lodge since stations are limited, and checking the Montana DOT website for closures.
- Summer weather is usually mild with average highs in the 60s-70s°F (15-21°C), though occasional thunderstorms can bring heavy rain and strong winds.
- The Beartooth Mountains are home to bears, elk, deer, and bighorn sheep; drivers should be extra cautious at dawn and dusk when animals are most active.
- The highway reaches elevations over 10,000 feet (3,000 meters), which can cause altitude sickness; take regular breaks, stay hydrated, and watch for symptoms.

### Grill safety guidelines @2023-05-28 [suggestion]
- Inspect the grill before use for damage, rust, or worn-out parts; ensure all connections are secure.
- Check that the propane tank is filled, in good condition, and not expired; follow manufacturer maintenance instructions.
- Place the grill on a level, stable surface away from flammable materials, overhanging branches, and dry leaves.
- Keep children and pets at least 3 feet away from the grill to avoid burns and heat exposure.
- Place the grill on a heat-resistant surface such as a grill mat or fireproof pad.
- Monitor wind direction to avoid sparks and embers blowing toward flammable materials.
- Never leave the grill unattended while it is in use.
- Use long-handled utensils to handle food and adjust the grill to avoid burns.
- Keep a fire extinguisher or a bucket of sand nearby in case of emergencies.
- Be careful when handling hot grease or oil, as it can cause serious burns.
- Do not overload the grill with too much food, as it can cause flare-ups and accidents.
- Turn off the grill when not in use and make sure it is cool before storing.
- Store the grill in a well-ventilated area away from flammable materials, out of reach of children and pets.
- Dispose of ash and waste properly, following the campsite's regulations.
- Familiarize yourself with campsite rules regarding portable grills and campfires.
- Check with local authorities for fire restrictions or burn bans in the area.
- Keep a bucket of water nearby, especially in areas prone to wildfires.
