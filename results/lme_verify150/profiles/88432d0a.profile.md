- Assistant advised reducing the yeast amount by 25–50% compared to a standard recipe with a slower rise, to prevent over-fermentation.

- Assistant suggested a coarser, flakier salt like Maldon or kosher salt to help control yeast growth during a slower rise.
- Assistant noted a slower rise develops more complex flavors, a stronger gluten structure for an open crumb, and breaks down starches for tenderness.
- Assistant warned delaying fermentation too long risks over-fermenting the dough, causing off-flavors, a dense crumb, and less appealing texture.
- Assistant advised using a stronger flour with higher protein content to support the longer fermentation time of a slower rise.
- Assistant noted steam is essential for a crispy crust; use a pan of water in the oven, spray the oven with water, or a steam injection system.
- Assistant advised scoring the bread before baking with a lame or razor blade to control expansion and create an even, crispy crust.
- Assistant advised baking bread on a preheated stone to absorb excess moisture and distribute heat evenly for a crispier crust.
- Assistant advised cooling bread on a wire rack after baking to help the crust set and become crisper.
