### Nitrogen Cycle & Water Parameters (assistant suggested)
- Assistant explained the nitrogen cycle converts toxic ammonia into nitrite via Nitrosomonas bacteria, then into less toxic nitrate via Nitrobacter bacteria (2023-05-20).
- Assistant explained nitrite is a byproduct of the nitrogen cycle where beneficial bacteria convert ammonia into nitrate; suggested adding a biological supplement or more biological media (2023-05-20).
- Assistant advised: if ammonia reads 0.25 ppm or higher, perform an immediate 50–75% water change and investigate the cause (2023-05-20).
- Assistant advised: if nitrite reads 0.5 ppm or higher, perform a 25–50% water change and investigate the cause (2023-05-20).
- Assistant advised: if nitrate reads 20 ppm or higher, perform larger 25–50% water changes and consider adding plants (2023-05-20).
- Assistant stated nitrate of 0–5 ppm is ideal for most fish, 5–10 ppm acceptable, and 10–20 ppm high (2023-05-20).
- Assistant explained fish produce ammonia as waste through their gills, urine, and feces; ammonia is the primary nitrogen source in an aquarium (2023-05-20).
- Assistant noted ammonia is highly toxic to fish and can cause rapid death even at low concentrations (2023-05-20).
- Assistant noted nitrite is toxic to fish and can cause damage to fish gills, kidneys, and liver (2023-05-20).
- Assistant noted high nitrate levels can contribute to algae growth and other water quality issues (2023-05-20).
- Assistant noted ammonia can spike during initial tank setup, after a large water change, if overstocked, or if biological filtration is inadequate (2023-05-20).
- Assistant advised adding plants to the aquarium helps absorb excess nutrients including nitrate (2023-05-20).
- Assistant advised, for high nitrite levels, performing more frequent water changes or considering increasing the tank's water volume (2023-05-20).
- Assistant recommended, for extremely high nitrite, a larger water change up to 25–30% to lower levels quickly (2023-05-20).
- Assistant advised investigating ammonia spike causes such as overfeeding, dead fish, or inadequate biological filtration (2023-05-20).
- Assistant advised keeping a record of water test results to track changes and identify potential issues (2023-05-20).
- Assistant advised testing water parameters in the morning before feeding for the most accurate readings (2023-05-20).
- Assistant advised regularly testing tank water for nitrite with a reliable test kit to track progress and adjust the water change schedule (2023-05-20).
- Assistant suggested nitrite-absorbing resins or zeolite filter media to remove nitrite from the water, following manufacturer instructions (2023-05-20).
