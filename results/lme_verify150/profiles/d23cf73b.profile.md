### Teff Flour Information
- Stated teff is an ancient grain native to Ethiopia and Eritrea, gluten-free, high in protein, and rich in fiber and iron @2023-05-21
- Stated teff flour has a dark brown to reddish-brown color and a slightly sweet, nutty, earthy flavor @2023-05-21
- Stated teff flour contains about 7-8% protein and is coarser than most other flours @2023-05-21
- Stated teff flour absorbs more liquid than other flours, so recipe liquid content may need adjustment @2023-05-21
- Stated teff flour resembles whole wheat flour in coarseness and nutty flavor, and quinoa flour in protein content @2023-05-21
- Advised storing teff flour in an airtight container in a cool, dry place and using it fresh @2023-05-21
- Explained teff is a pseudo-cereal—a flowering plant producing a grain-like fruit, not a true cereal @2023-05-21
- Stated teff flour can be used 1:1 with other flours but the ratio may need adjustment depending on recipe and desired texture @2023-05-21
- Advised mixing teff flour thoroughly to avoid lumps as it can be quite dense @2023-05-21
- Warned that old or stale teff flour can be bitter and affect the final product @2023-05-21

### Saffron Syrup Guidance
- Saffron-infused simple syrup uses 1 cup sugar, 1 cup water, 1/2 teaspoon saffron threads (about 10-12 threads) @2023-05-22
- Instructed soaking saffron in 1 tbsp hot water (90-100°F) for 5-10 min; combine sugar and water over medium heat; add saffron mixture; simmer 5-7 min; strain and cool to room temperature @2023-05-22
- Saffron-infused simple syrup can be stored in the refrigerator for up to 2 weeks @2023-05-22
- Saffron Gin Fizz recipe: 1 1/2 oz gin, 1/2 oz saffron syrup, 1/2 oz lemon juice, splash of soda water, garnished with saffron thread and lemon twist @2023-05-22
- For a double batch (2 cups syrup), 20-25 saffron threads (1-1.25 teaspoons) are needed @2023-05-22
- For a triple batch (3 cups syrup), 30-35 saffron threads (1.5-1.75 teaspoons) are needed @2023-05-22
- For a quadruple batch (4 cups syrup), 40-45 saffron threads (2-2.25 teaspoons) are needed @2023-05-22
- Advised using high-quality saffron threads for best flavor and color; can add lemon peel or cinnamon stick while simmering; syrup is versatile for cocktails, desserts, and savory dishes @2023-05-22

### Naan Bread Guidance
- Naan recipe provided: 1 1/2 cups atta flour, 1/4 tsp salt, 1/4 tsp sugar, 1/2 tsp active dry yeast, 1/2 cup lukewarm water, 2 tbsp melted ghee or butter; yields 6-8 naan @2023-05-22
- Naan bread traditionally uses atta (chapati) flour, a finely milled whole wheat flour; all-purpose flour can be substituted @2023-05-22
- Naan dough should rest for at least 1-2 hours or overnight in the refrigerator to develop gluten @2023-05-22
- Naan should be cooked in a very hot oven (500°F) using a hot skillet or preheated baking stone to mimic a tandoor @2023-05-22
- Naan bread should be brushed with melted butter or ghee for rich, nutty flavor and golden color @2023-05-22
- Naan bread relies on active dry yeast or instant yeast for its signature softness and puffiness @2023-05-22
- Advised not overworking or over-kneading the dough; mix just until ingredients come together to avoid dense, tough naan @2023-05-22
- Advised kneading dough 5-7 minutes until smooth and elastic; preheat baking stone at least 30 minutes; cook naan 1-2 minutes until puffed with blisters @2023-05-22
