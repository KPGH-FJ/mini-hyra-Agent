- Assistant noted: the Les Paul is not ideal for acoustic-like fingerpicking — humbucker tone is too thick, sustain rings too long, action is higher, and the neck is wider than an acoustic's (2023-05-21).
- Assistant noted: standard tuning is EADGBE (low to high); open D tuning is DADF#AD (low to high), offering a warmer, more resonant tone focused on lower frequencies (2023-05-21).
- Assistant noted: open D's intervals are mostly perfect fifths with simpler, more consistent chord shapes; standard tuning mixes perfect fourths and major thirds with more complex chord shapes (2023-05-21).
- Assistant noted: open D tuning is well-suited to slide guitar and to folk, blues, and roots music with a laid-back, rhythmic approach; standard tuning is less conducive to slide (2023-05-21).
- Assistant suggested: for open D on a Les Paul, use heavier string gauges (0.012-0.052 or 0.013-0.056) for a fuller, more resonant tone (2023-05-21).
- Assistant suggested: for open D on a Les Paul — adjust action and intonation for the lower registers and lower the pickup height to avoid a harsh, bright sound (2023-05-21).
- Assistant suggested: for open D on a Les Paul — gain around 5-6, boost bass at 100-150 Hz, cut midrange at 500-800 Hz, reduce treble at 5k-8k Hz (2023-05-21).
- Assistant suggested: for open D on a Les Paul — use the neck pickup, play with a relaxed right hand, and add compression for even dynamics (2023-05-21).
